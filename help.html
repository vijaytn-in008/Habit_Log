Product Positioning

The PivotMe Execution Framework

PivotMe is for a person who is genuinely honest with themselves about how they use their time, what they do, what they avoid, and what actually happens.

It is not about pretending to be productive or collecting data for the sake of tracking. The purpose is to understand reality, learn from evidence, and make better adjustments. It records what actually happens without guilt, streaks, or penalties.

Core Purpose:

“PivotMe helps me build a system, execute it, record what actually happened, learn from my own evidence, and adjust the system honestly.”

What PivotMe Is
  • A deliberate personal system connecting planning, execution, logging, and reflection
  • An objective, factual record of your actual choices, timestamps, and real follow-through
  • A time-blocking engine where you commit finite hours rather than maintaining infinite wishlists
  • A private, local-first tool that stores data on your device and your personal Google Sheet
  • A diagnostic system that treats missed plans as evidence for adjustment rather than personal failure
What PivotMe Is NOT
  • Not just a habit tracker focused on fragile streaks
  • Not just a task manager or endless backlog checklist
  • Not just a calendar
  • Not just a timer
  • Not a motivation app or vanity dashboard
  • Not an AI that tells you what your personality or psychology is
Core Engine

The PivotMe Execution Loop

PivotMe operates around an ongoing 7-stage feedback loop designed to test intentions against real evidence:

DECIDE → PLAN → EXECUTE → LOG → REFLECT → ADJUST → REPEAT
1
DECIDE

Determine your growth habits, quit habits, and concrete tasks that align with your priorities.

2
PLAN

Allocate attention across the day, week, or month. Schedule dedicated time blocks on your timeline.

3
EXECUTE

Carry out the planned work using Focus Mode or Parallel Mode.

4
LOG

Record what actually happened, including useful context (before activity, physical state, mental state, exact time).

5
REFLECT

Analyze accumulated evidence and identify patterns from your own history without psychological conclusions.

6
ADJUST

Change the system based on what the evidence shows—calibrating fallbacks, replacements, step sizes, or schedules.

7
REPEAT

Run the adjusted system again. Consistent logging produces better evidence for the next cycle.

System Architecture & Core Definitions

To keep logging lightweight and reflections accurate, PivotMe uses precise, practical concepts:

GROWTH HABIT

A behavior you want to build or perform consistently.
Outcomes: Done, Partially Done, Skipped.

FALLBACK

A reduced version of a Growth Habit used when the normal target cannot be completed (e.g. Target: Study for 2 hours; Fallback: Study for 20 minutes).

QUIT HABIT

A behavior you want to stop or reduce.
Outcomes: Resisted, Partially Resisted, Done.

REPLACEMENT ACTIVITY

An alternative activity configured for a Quit Habit and used when resisting the behavior.

TASK

The outcome you want to accomplish.

NEXT ACTION

A small, concrete action that moves a Task forward.

PLAN

When you intend to give your attention to tasks and habits across the day, week, or month.

FOCUS SESSION

An optional focused execution session connected to planned work.

LOG

Records what actually happened, including useful context.

REFLECTION

Analyzes accumulated evidence and identifies patterns from your own history.

ADJUST

Changes the system based on what the evidence shows.

Task Execution Rule:

If a Task has Next Actions, you normally execute and log the Next Action rather than repeatedly logging the parent Task. The Task represents the desired overall outcome, while Next Actions are the concrete execution steps.

System Architecture

Tasks vs. Habits: When to Use Which

A common failure in personal systems is confusing recurring routines with concrete project deliverables. PivotMe maintains a deliberate distinction between the two, while unifying them on the daily Plan timeline.

Habits (Behavioral Baselines)

Habits are recurring behavioral patterns and routines that repeat on regular schedules or specific triggers. Their purpose is to establish an automatic behavioral default.

  • No final completion date: Practiced continuously (daily, weekdays, weekly).
  • Two distinct categories: Positive routines to nurture (Growth) or impulses to eliminate (Quit).
  • Examples: Morning meditation, 30-minute reading, gym workout, avoiding late-night social media bingeing.

Tasks (Bounded Deliverables)

Tasks are bounded, actionable outcomes or work items. Once their specific objective is accomplished, the task is finished and archived.

  • Definite end-state: Has clear completion criteria.
  • Structured execution: Broken down into sequential Next Actions you can execute immediately.
  • Examples: Prepare Q3 presentation deck, file tax documents, repair kitchen faucet, write project proposal.
Where They Meet: The Plan Timeline

You do not execute habits in a vacuum and tasks in a silo. Both demand slices of your finite daily attention. The Plan page is where both habits and tasks converge into a single, cohesive schedule for your day.

Daily Engine

Plan: The Time-Blocking Engine

Plan is where you decide when you intend to give your attention across the day, week, or month. It is not an infinite task backlog; it is a finite schedule of your attention.

Why Planning is Separate from a Task List

A task backlog is an inventory of things you could do. Looking at 30 tasks generates decision fatigue and delay. The Plan is an explicit commitment to what you will do today and when. If an item is not on the Plan, you are not working on it today.

Why Rescheduling is Tracked, Not Erased

When an unexpected delay happens, tapping Alter Plan shifts the time slot and records the shift in a visible history trail (e.g. 09:00 → 11:30) with an optional reason. Erasing changes conceals systemic optimism; tracking shifts provides the exact diagnostic data needed to calibrate realistic planning.

Key Capabilities of the Plan Page

Dedicated Time Slots & Presets

Assign exact start and end times, or use quick presets (Now 30m, Now 1h, Morning 09:00, Afternoon 14:00, Evening 19:00) to schedule blocks in seconds.

Execution Mode Selection

Choose Focus Mode (default, single-tasking) or Parallel Mode (intentional background tracking) when adding items.

Context & Sub-Goal Notes

Add optional context or a specific sub-goal to any planned block to clarify exactly what "done" means for that session.

Actual vs. Planned Comparison

Completed plan cards display a direct comparison between your planned time and the actual logged execution timestamp and duration.

Plan History & Weekly Review

Preserves what was actually planned and what happened to those plans. Weekly review helps you understand your planning behavior over time.

Move to Tomorrow

Cleanly defer uncompleted items to tomorrow's plan with a single tap when emergency priorities supersede your agenda.

Plan is Not Another Reflection System:

Plan manages when you intend to schedule your attention and tracks your planning behavior. Reflection analyzes empirical execution logs (what actually happened, context, and friction). They serve distinct, complementary roles in the loop.

Execution Mastery

Execution: Focus vs. Parallel

How you execute turns intent into action. PivotMe provides two execution modes and an optional focus session timer.

Focus Mode (Default)

Single-Task Immersion

Focus is the foundational default for all deep work, habits, and cognitive tasks. When a Focus block is active, the app highlights your current priority and de-emphasizes the rest of the day. Single-tasking eliminates context-switching penalties and ensures high-quality execution.

Parallel Mode

Intentional Background Tracking

Parallel execution is permitted only for activities that naturally do not compete for active cognitive attention—such as running a washing machine while reading, or listening to an educational podcast while on a walk. Multitasking on deep work is an illusion; parallel mode should be intentional, rare, and restricted to passive processes.

The Focus Execution Session (Optional):

Starting work can have friction. The built-in stopwatch timer anchors your attention on the current block as an optional focused execution session. Using the timer is completely optional—you can always log directly when finished. When you do use it, tapping Finish & Log records your actual duration and timestamp directly into your ground-truth logs.

Tasks Guide

Tasks & Progressive Execution

Tasks in PivotMe keep execution concrete by focusing on outcomes and next actions. You do not have to map out a complex project hierarchy before taking action.

Task vs Next Action

TASK

The overall outcome you want to accomplish (e.g. "Build my portfolio website" or "Learning web development"). Holds the deadline, priority, and target objective.

NEXT ACTION

A small, concrete step you can take to move the Task forward (e.g. "Sketch the homepage" or "Complete DOM lesson"). Executable in a single focused session.

Example Structure:

Task: Build my portfolio website
Next Actions:
  • Sketch the homepage
  • Create the HTML structure
  • Build the navigation
  • Add the projects section

"You do not need to plan everything before starting. Create one Next Action you can do now and add more as the work becomes clearer."

Execution & Logging Paths

Simple Task:
Task → Log Task
For standalone deliverables with no separate actions. Directly log what happened when done.
Task with Next Actions:
Task → Next Action → Log Next Action
The Next Action is the execution unit. Execute and log the step directly without having to log the parent Task every time.
Evidence for Reflection:

Logging records what actually happened (Done, Partially Done, Skipped, physical and mental states, alternative activities, interruptions). This gives Reflection factual evidence to uncover real execution patterns without psychological guesswork.

Logging & Preset Suggestions

Strictly Three Ground-Truth Outcomes

Tasks and Next Actions log into strictly Done, Partially Done, or Skipped. Every session records actual behavioral ground truth without guilt or punitive penalties.

Preset Chips & Custom Input

Log quickly by tapping contextual preset chips for Physical State, Mental State, Location, and Activity Before. Frequent entries automatically float to the front, and custom free text is always supported.

Non-Destructive Deadlines & Task Actions

Deadline Extension Audit Trail

Extend deadlines through the Task Actions card on the Task page or directly during Partial/Skipped logging. Your original deadline is permanently preserved, and every extension date with its reason is recorded in a transparent history audit.

Remove After Completion

When "Remove after completion" is enabled, completed tasks and next actions are hidden from active views to keep focus uncluttered, while remaining fully accessible and restorable under "Show Completed".

Habits Guide

Habits: Growth, Quit & Outcomes

PivotMe treats building positive behaviors and stopping destructive habits as distinct behavioral tracks, each with three explicit outcomes.

Growth Habits

Behaviors you want to build or perform consistently (e.g. Study, Workout, Reading).

Done: Completed target action as planned.
Partially Done: Made partial progress, with the option to log a configured Fallback action.
Skipped: Did another activity instead of the planned habit.
Fallback Action: A reduced version of the same habit configured directly in the habit and shown naturally during "Partially Done" logging.

Quit Habits

Behaviors you want to stop or reduce (e.g. Late Night Scrolling, Mindless Snacking).

Resisted: Urge resisted, with quick selection of configured Replacement Activity.
Partially Resisted: Delayed or partially curbed the urge.
Done: The unwanted quit behavior occurred.
Replacement Activity: The constructive alternative configured for a Quit Habit and made accessible during Resisted and Partially Resisted logging.
Objective Outcomes Without Moralizing:

Quit Habits use three factual outcomes: Resisted, Partially Resisted, and Done (the behavior occurred). Recording that a quit behavior occurred is neutral empirical data needed to detect preceding activities and physical/mental states, not an invitation for guilt or failure spirals.

Data Integrity

Logging: Ground Truth & Context

A plan is a statement of intent; a log records what actually happened. The value of PivotMe comes from being honest and consistent with logging. Better evidence produces more useful reflection.

What Logging Captures:

Preset choices make logging fast, while custom answers allow more detail. Logging can capture what happened, what happened before, physical state, mental state, place, time, interruptions, and what you engaged in instead when something was skipped.

Growth Habit Evidence

  • Done: What did you do? What did you do before? Physical state, mental state, exact date/time.
  • Partially Done: What did you actually do? Fallback action indicator. What did you do before? Physical & mental states.
  • Skipped: What did you do instead? What did you do before? Physical & mental states.

Quit Habit Evidence

  • Resisted: What did you do instead? Replacement action selector. What happened before? Physical & mental states.
  • Partially Resisted: What happened / what did you actually do? Replacement indicator. Before activity, physical & mental states.
  • Done: What did you do? What happened before? Physical state, mental state, exact date/time.
Flexibility in Logging:

You do not need to record every detail every time. Tapping quick preset chips takes only seconds. Add custom detail when something unexpected happens or when friction is notable. Any honest record of reality is far more valuable than skipping logging.

Reflection Guide

Reflection: Patterns & System Calibration

Reflection analyzes accumulated evidence and identifies patterns from your own history. The value comes from being honest and consistent with logging: better evidence produces more useful reflection.

"What helps me?"

Identifies environments, times of day, and weekdays where your completion rate is highest.

"What gets in my way?"

Highlights recurring friction triggers (e.g. fatigue, phone notifications, unplanned meetings).

Multi-Dimensional Drill-Down

Filter your execution logs by date range, specific weekday, habit/task, location, or status to isolate specific bottlenecks.

Adjust Systems, Not Blame

When an item struggles, do not blame yourself. Shrink the step size, change the execution time, or redesign the environment.

Correlation vs. Causation: Strict Empirical Safeguards

PivotMe does not claim that a correlation is automatically a cause. Reflections should be treated as observations from your own history, not absolute psychological conclusions. The system identifies empirical associations in your recorded data (requiring a minimum sample size of n ≥ 5) so you can make informed adjustments to your system honestly.

Security & Data

Local-First Architecture & Data Ownership

PivotMe is built with a transparent, local-first architecture. You own your data completely—there are no proprietary servers, tracking cookies, or walled gardens.

Local-First Storage (IndexedDB)

Every habit, task, plan, and log is stored instantly inside your browser's private IndexedDB database. The app works fully offline without any network connection.

Personal Google Sheets Cloud Sync

When connected, data synchronizes directly with your personal Google Sheet via a script running in your Google account. Your data is readable in standard spreadsheet rows.

Full JSON Export & Import

In the Settings modal, use Export Data to download a complete, unencrypted JSON backup of your entire database, or use Import Data to restore or migrate devices.

Clear Data Options

Settings provides clear, irreversible options to reset local storage, clear the service worker cache, or disconnect your Google Sheet whenever you choose.

Essential Security Rules:
  • Never share your Apps Script Web App URL publicly. In this architecture, that URL serves as the private connection credential allowing access to read and write your spreadsheet.
  • Never share your Google Sheet URL or ID publicly. Keep your spreadsheet private within your Google Drive.
  • Never paste personal passwords or sensitive credentials into generic script templates or notes fields.
  • Client-Side Code Visibility: The frontend application is open, client-side code running in your web browser. True security comes from your Google account's permissions boundary and keeping your deployment URL private—never from attempting to obscure client-side JavaScript.
  • Realistic Privacy: We do not make absolute, exaggerated claims such as "Your data can never be stolen." Security is a shared responsibility between your device security, your Google account security (e.g., 2FA), and keeping your Web App URL confidential.
Maintenance

Updating Google Apps Script

When a new version of PivotMe is released with backend improvements, follow this 9-step update process without losing your historical records.

1

Read the Release Note

Check the release notes to see what was improved or added in the updated backend script.

2

Open Existing Apps Script

Open the Google Sheet connected to your PivotMe app and click Extensions → Apps Script. Do not create a new sheet!

3

Back Up Your Sheet (Optional)

Inside Google Sheets, click File → Make a copy if you want a local backup before updating.

4

Replace Script Code

Select all code in Code.gs, replace it with the new version of appscript.gs from the Setup section above.

5

Save the Project

Press Ctrl + S (or Cmd + S) to save your updated code.

6

Deploy New Version

Click Deploy → Manage deployments → click the pencil (Edit) icon → under Version select New version → click Deploy.

7

Keep Your Existing Spreadsheet

PivotMe's backend script automatically detects missing columns or new tabs and adds them on first sync. Your existing habit logs and task data are preserved intact.

8

Return to PivotMe Settings

If your Web App URL remained the same, no URL change is needed. If a new deployment URL was generated, paste it into Settings.

9

Test Connection

Use the Test Connection tool in the Setup section to verify that your updated deployment is responding with status: ok.

When is Data Migration Actually Required?

You do NOT need to create a new spreadsheet for updates. The script is written to be backward-compatible: it inspects your existing columns and appends any newly introduced headers without touching your data rows. Manual migration is only required in the rare event of a major structural schema redesign, which will be clearly flagged in the release notes.

Architecture

App Updates vs. Apps Script Updates

To maintain your application confidently, understand the distinction between frontend application updates and backend script updates.

Updating PivotMe (Frontend)

  • Updates HTML, CSS, and client-side JavaScript files.
  • Managed automatically by your browser via the Service Worker cache whenever you load the app online.
  • Does not erase your local IndexedDB database.
  • Does not alter your Google Sheet structure.

Updating Apps Script (Backend)

  • Changes server-side logic inside your Google account.
  • Requires manually pasting the updated code and creating a New Version deployment in Apps Script.
  • Handles how data is written to and read from your Google Sheet tabs.
  • Maintains your existing spreadsheet records across versions.
Hosting Migration

Moving PivotMe to Another Host

Because PivotMe uses a decoupled, static architecture, moving where the frontend is hosted is straightforward and safe.

Frontend Hosting Layer

Static files hosted on GitHub Pages, Cloudflare Pages, Vercel, or a private local server.

User Data Layer

Local IndexedDB + your private Google Apps Script Web App + your Google Sheet.

What Happens When You Move Hosts?
  • Your Google Sheet and Apps Script do not change. They remain untouched in your Google Drive.
  • IndexedDB is origin-isolated: Browsers isolate IndexedDB by domain. When you open PivotMe on a new host domain, the browser starts with a blank local database.
  • How to populate the new host: Simply open Settings on the new host, paste your existing Google Apps Script Web App URL, and tap Restore from Sheet in Settings. All your habits, tasks, logs, reflections, and plans will immediately download to your new device/host!
Troubleshooting

Troubleshooting Common Issues

Quick, practical diagnostic steps for common connection or sync scenarios.

Connection Failed / Sync Indicator Red
  1. Use the Test Connection tool in the Setup section to check the exact error response.
  2. Check your Web App URL: it must end in /exec (not /edit).
  3. In Google Apps Script, click Deploy → Manage deployments. Ensure Who has access is set to Anyone (if set to "Only myself", browser fetch calls will fail).
  4. Verify your device has an active internet connection.
Data Not Appearing in Google Sheet
  1. Check the header sync indicator. If it shows yellow (syncing) or red (offline), wait until your connection resumes.
  2. Remember: changes are saved to your local IndexedDB immediately and queued for background upload.
  3. Ensure the Web App URL in your Settings corresponds to the Google Sheet you are currently inspecting.
  4. Refresh the spreadsheet in your browser.
Changed Browser, Device, or Incognito Window
  1. IndexedDB is local to the specific browser and device. A new browser or device will start empty by design.
  2. Open Settings in the new browser, paste your Web App URL, and tap Restore from Sheet to download your cloud records.
Updated Apps Script But Still Showing Old Behavior
  1. In Google Apps Script editor, you must click Deploy → Manage deployments → Edit → select New version from the dropdown → click Deploy.
  2. Simply pressing Save in the code editor does not update the active Web App deployment. You must create a new version deployment!
